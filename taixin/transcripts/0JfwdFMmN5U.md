## 口播内容（英文转述）

Many students often come to ask me, "Teacher, why is it that every time I visit a client, I try so hard to find a topic to warm up the atmosphere, but the other party always coldly replies with 'Oh, thank you' and then stops talking?" Actually, it's because we treat brown-nosing as chatting. Back when I was selling Porsches, I encountered the same bottleneck. Later, I asked a top salesperson how on earth to close the distance with a big boss. The senior told me pointedly... ...the praise a boss has heard is more than the salt you've eaten. If you say he's great, he feels absolutely nothing. Then he passed on a communication formula worth a fortune. If you want to open a boss's chatterbox, you must first learn to replace praise with details and replace compliments with seeking advice. I used this technique to sell a 6-million-dollar Porsche. If we only say, 'Boss, your company is great, your management is amazing,' it's empty nonsense. We should find specific scenes and details... ...because details show you've observed carefully; this is the highest form of sincerity. Use seeking advice instead of praise. When you meet an impressive person or thing, don't just say 'Wow' in admiration; instead, ask questions in the form of seeking advice because everyone loves to play the mentor and wants to help others, especially entrepreneurs at the top of the pyramid. The next day, I went to visit Boss Lin, and it came in handy immediately. That day, I walked into Boss Lin's office and noticed a detail: every single one of his employees, from the receptionist to the passing assistants, would actively stop to ask me if I needed help, and they were always smiling and nodding. Their attitude was excellent. So, when I entered Boss Lin's office, if I followed my old habits, I would probably say 'Boss Lin, the quality of your employees is really good, impressive.' You can imagine, Boss Lin would probably just reply, 'Oh, it's nothing, it's expected,' and then stop talking. But I immediately switched to the formula my senior taught me. After I sat down, I looked at Boss Lin and said sincerely, 'Boss Lin, I just walked in and noticed a very deep detail. Every one of your employees greets customers proactively and their attitude is elegant, not rushed at all. This is truly not simple. May I ask how you trained them?' This sentence was simply a stroke of genius. Boss Lin, who originally had an expressionless face ready to haggle with me, his eyes lit up immediately. The conversation box opened directly. From how he started his business to his criteria for selecting people, and then to how he personally leads internal meetings and training every month, he spoke with great excitement. I sat opposite him, listening attentively, nodding occasionally, and continuing to ask him questions following his words. At that moment, I knew we had established a connection of trust. I sold a 6-million-dollar Porsche that day. So I finally understood that sales isn't about how beautifully you speak, but about speaking to the person's heart. Want to see more?
