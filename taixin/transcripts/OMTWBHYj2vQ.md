When the customer tells you, 'I know your boss, can you make it cheaper?', never say 'Then call the boss yourself.' It feels good but loses the order. I met Mr. Wang at the showroom. We discussed features and maintenance, took a test drive, and returned to the desk to view the quote. He tapped the table and said, 'Tai Xin, I actually know your boss. Can you make this price more attractive?' Every salesperson faces this. Your first thought is 'Really? Why didn't you say so before? Are you just trying to haggle with me?' If I were younger, I might say, 'Well, ask the boss then,' or 'Even if the boss comes, it is still this price.' Logically these are correct, but sales is not a debate. The more right you are, the harder it is for the client to back down. He mentions the boss to get respect. He wants to know if I treat him as an important person. I didn't expose him. I said: 'I understand. Since you know the boss, I absolutely cannot casually serve you. You are my VIP.' He leaned in because I didn't insult him. I told him I didn't use a high-price tactic for him because he's a friend of the boss. The point isn't that it can't be cheaper, but that I respect you and didn't treat you like an outsider. I said: 'I can't mess with price, but I'll arrange exclusive car delivery and maintenance service for you, which ordinary customers don't get.' He asked if the price was really firm. I said if I cut it just to close, you'd worry I had more room I didn't give you. I want you to buy with peace of mind. Stable price, great service. He paused, then told me to write it down. The order closed. No magic tricks, just one sequence: Give face first, uphold the rules, then use service to replace price cuts. Most fail here because they either fold or get defensive. If you fold, the quote looks dishonest. If you're hard, the client feels insulted. True closing is: I understand your need for status, but my rules are firm. Firm stance, gentle attitude, better service. Let the client decide with dignity. I've compiled a 'Face-Saving Closing' cheat sheet. Comment 'face' for a copy. Close high-value deals with me.
